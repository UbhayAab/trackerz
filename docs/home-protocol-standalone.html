The week

Why it's built this way

Every muscle gets touched 4–6 times a week, but only one heavy exposure per movement pattern. The rest is medium and pump work. That's what makes daily training survivable — not the fact that the exercise names change.

Rotating exercises buys you joint variety, skill spread, and boredom insurance. It does not buy you recovery. Your pecs don't know whether you pressed flat or incline; they only know how much hard work landed on them.

Progression under a hard load cap

Double progression: hit the top of the rep range on every set for two sessions running, then move up. When the dumbbells run out, you climb this ladder instead. Each rung is a real increase in stimulus at the same 25 kg.

  1. Add reps within the prescribed range.
  2. Add a set (cap: 5 working sets per exercise).
  3. Slow the eccentric to 3–4 seconds.
  4. Add a 1–2 second pause at the hardest position.
  5. Cut rest by 15 seconds.
  6. 1.5 reps — full rep, then half rep, count as one.
  7. Myo-reps: one hard set, rest 15s, do 3–5 more, repeat ×4.
  8. Go unilateral — one arm or one leg carries the same load.
  9. Extend the range — deficit push-ups, deep split squats, elevated heels.
  10. Change leverage — decline → archer → pseudo-planche; pull-ups → archer → weighted.

Legs are the real constraint

50 kg total is heavy for pressing and light for squatting. Your legs will outgrow the dumbbells almost immediately, so leg work in this program is built on single-leg positions, slow tempo, pauses, and higher reps rather than load. Bulgarian split squats, single-leg RDLs, and step-ups will be your heavy leg work for the foreseeable future. That's not a compromise — done properly they're brutal.

Running it on a deficit

You're eating under maintenance, so recovery is the scarce resource, not effort. Two rules: keep the heavy sets to 1–2 reps in reserve, never grinding failure on the compounds, and take failure only on the pump work where the joints aren't loaded.

Every fourth week, cut all working sets by roughly a third and keep the loads the same. You'll come back stronger, not detrained.

If sleep is short or the day was brutal, run the session at medium intensity instead of skipping. A 60% session beats a missed one — that's the whole reason you moved training home.

Session mechanics

Rest. Heavy 150s · Medium 90s · Pump 45–60s · Core 45s.

Warm-up, 6 minutes. 30 band pull-aparts, 20 bodyweight squats, 10 push-ups, 10 arm circles each way, then two ramp-up sets on the first exercise.

Time. Days A–E run 50–60 minutes. Day F runs 35. If you're short on time, cut from the bottom of the list, never the top.

Order is not decorative. The first two exercises are the ones that drive adaptation. Everything below is accessory.
